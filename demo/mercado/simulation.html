Each team begins with two copies of its card; only the spare is eligible to sell. Reserve: 100 P. Per-trade limit: 200 P. Hourly limit: 500 P. The simulator explicitly authorizes bounded autonomy and partial completion.

What this demonstrates—and what it doesn’t

The existing Python lead detector, route finder, private-value checks, approvals, persistent human gates and local execution agents produce these traces. The synthetic API supplies ownership, fees and settlements. No production trading policy was changed for the demo.

The baseline replays the supplied failed conversations with a counterparty that cannot supply the requested card. It does not model every possible bilateral cash deal. The fixtures keep receipt values fixed and do not model page-completion bonuses. These cases show that coordination can unlock safe trades from those leads; they do not establish that triangulation beats every alternative or improves every economy.

Execution is not atomic. A rejection before all approvals prevents trading. A withdrawal or value change after approval can leave a partially completed route. Already settled trades are not reversed.

Replay is deterministic with seed 16. Route nonces are deterministic only in the offline harness; production randomness remains unchanged. The source bundle includes the existing implementation, synthetic test API and assertions for cash/card conservation, positive settled-leg benefit and expected scenario outcomes.
