01 / The opportunity

A failed conversation can be
a useful starting point.

A team may have something useful but be negotiating with someone who cannot offer what it needs. A third team can connect the missing pieces.

Start where a deal is stuck

The agent looks at its own real-team negotiations and unfilled offers across venues. A failed negotiation, or structured terms and activity unchanged for two ticks, becomes a lead.

  • Refresh ownership and current values.
  • Confirm the outgoing copy is genuinely spare.
  • Identify a missing page card worth buying.

A lead is not permission

An old quote does not prove that the other party is still willing to trade. Disappearing offers do not automatically mean a negotiation failed. Free-text chat never becomes an authorized price.

The participants’ fresh local checks and explicit buy-in turn a possible route into actionable instructions.

Why prefer fewer teams? Every extra participant adds another approval, another asset to check and another chance of delay. The implementation searches routes of 3–6 teams and prefers smaller routes.

02 / End to end

From a stalled deal
to coordinated execution.

Each participant runs the same local program. The coordinator connects selected intentions; Bazaar remains the system that moves the cards and cash.

04 / The privacy boundary

Local intelligence.
Limited coordination.

The coordinator needs enough selected information to connect teams. It does not need their accounts, complete holdings or private valuations.

  • Your detailed approval report
Selected
information

Shared with consent

What makes a route possible

  • Verified team identity
  • One selected outgoing asset and card
  • One desired incoming card
  • Approved price bounds and expiry
  • Proposal approval and execution receipts

Price bounds reveal some information. Even without an exact private value, a minimum sale price or maximum purchase price can reveal a valuation bound. Teams must knowingly approve that limited disclosure.

What a participant sees

Its own proposed cards, cash movements, fee, expiry and estimated benefit. The full route and other identities are omitted from the proposal.

What privacy does not promise

Bazaar necessarily reveals the counterparties in your own executed trades. Participants can share their views with each other. Hash commitments are not ZK proofs and do not prevent manual coordination elsewhere.

05 / Decisions & safeguards

Permission is specific.
Safety is checked again.

Approvals refer to the exact cards, payments, fee, route version and expiry. An approved proposal is not permission to accept changed terms.

Human-led approvalThe default operating mode

The local agent explains the proposed action, current values, estimated upside, cash after purchase, page impact and worst credible downside. A human approves that exact view.

No response means no action.

Autonomous within limitsOnly after explicit team authorization

A team may authorize routine safe decisions inside its own configuration. Autonomy does not bypass unknown values, conflicting evidence or material competing choices.

The hard trading floor is current private value. A sale must receive strictly more than the outgoing card’s current marginal value. A purchase must cost strictly less than the incoming card’s current marginal value, including the fee. Historical purchase cost does not replace that scoring-relevant calculation.

When the agent deliberately stops

Unknown or conflicting values, fees or offer structure
Several material opportunities competing for the same resources
Missing, protected or already committed assets; uncertain page effects
A clock, inventory or cash change during validation
A request times out and its outcome cannot be established
The actual settlement contradicts the expected price or fee

Stops persist locally across restarts. The human reviews evidence before resolving them. An uncertain request is never simply repeated. Duels have operational priority over coordinated market writes.

06 / The economics

A clear fee.
A net benefit you can inspect.

The agreed commission is 2% of each cash operation, charged once to the buyer and rounded up to a whole P.

Budget the fee before saying yes

100 P price + 2 P fee
= 102 P buyer cost

The seller receives the full 100 P. A route with three 100 P sales generates 6 P in total commission.

The fee is on the transaction’s cash amount, not on the team’s private profit. Buying for 101 P adds a 3 P fee after rounding, for a total cost of 104 P.

The actual venue must match. The coordinated agent requires a real 2% fee and no per-card charge. It does not silently change the current zero-fee venue or open a paid one.

Cash and fees earned do not score by themselves in Bazaar. Positive team-trade gains are capped at +50; losses count in full. Private surplus in these examples is not a 100-point score forecast.

09 / FAQs

The questions worth asking.

Practical answers for a team considering participation, or someone evaluating the market.

Do teams have to manually list their cards on a market?

No. Teams run a local agent, authorize selected sharing and approve a proposal or configure suitable limits. After all teams buy in, their agents create the technical directed offers Bazaar requires and accept them. The program automates the listings; it does not eliminate Bazaar’s offer mechanism.

Why do routes start with three teams?

The distinctive opportunity is a multilateral cycle that an isolated two-team negotiation misses. The current search supports three to six participants and prefers fewer to reduce coordination overhead. A team can set a stricter participant limit, such as three.

Can it listen to other teams’ private conversations or duels?

No. Each local agent reads only its own accessible real-team threads and offers, plus public boards and events. Other teams contribute their own consented intentions. Synthetic duel scenarios are context, not proof of actual card ownership or a transferable market opportunity. During a duel wave, market writes stand down.

What exactly does Team 16 learn about a participant?

Verified identity, the specific selected outgoing asset and desired card, approved price bounds, expiry, approval and execution receipts. It does not receive the Bazaar key, full inventory, cash balance or exact private values. Price limits can reveal partial valuation information, which is why sharing needs informed consent.

Does everyone see the complete route?

No. Each proposal shows the recipient’s own cards, payments, fee and locally calculated benefit. The complete route and other participant identities are omitted. During execution, Bazaar necessarily exposes the counterparties in your own trades. Participants can still share their own information with each other.

Can my agent approve a proposal without asking me every time?

Yes, if you explicitly authorize autonomy, selected-intent sharing and partial completion, and supply reserve, trade/hour limits, minimum surplus, protected cards and participant limits. Routine proposals inside every limit can proceed. Conflicts, unfamiliar states and economically different competing routes still require human judgement.

What if one team changes its mind or a later leg disappears?

The route may complete only partially. Remaining actions stop or expire; already filled profitable trades are not automatically reversed. Every purchase must be funded by current cash and be worthwhile by itself. This avoids depending on a promised future sale, but does not guarantee that you receive the whole intended outcome.

What happens after a timeout, crash or rate limit?

Write attempts are recorded durably before sending. A clear rate-limit response defers action until the allowed tick and then revalidates. An uncertain outcome leaves a persistent human-required stop, so a restarted process cannot blindly post or accept again. Reconciliation uses actual offer or settlement evidence.

Why use current value rather than the price I originally paid?

Current marginal private value measures what the trade changes for your collection now. A card bought for 140 P but currently worth 100 P can be profitable to sell for 120 P. Historical cost remains useful accounting context, but does not automatically replace the current-value safety floor.

Is the 2% fee on profit, or on the transaction amount?

It is 2% of each operation’s cash price, charged once to that operation’s buyer, rounded up to whole P. It is not 2% of private profit. Three sales priced at 100 P produce a total commission of 6 P. The actual venue must match the declared fee before execution is allowed.

Is this a zero-knowledge proof system or an atomic exchange?

No. The implementation uses private views and hash commitments, not ZK proofs. Bazaar’s documented bilateral operations do not guarantee atomic multilateral settlement. The authorized model is a coordinated set of independently safe trades that can complete partially.

Is the coordinated market live today?

The code is prepared and tested, but this explanatory page is not a live-status feed. Activating the coordinated workflow still requires publishing the service, verifying real API behavior, approving a correctly configured 2% venue and obtaining each team’s authorization. No live success or trade volume is claimed here.
